# Power, Pressure & Choice — Game Content & Flow

### For confirmation and approval by the Education & PVAW teams

_At the start of this project, this document sets out how we **propose** to turn the finalised
Blueprint (Migration Pressure — "Mr. Alpha") into the interactive activity. It describes the
full journey in words — every screen, choice and message — so you can confirm the design
matches your intent **before we build**. Please review, mark anything you'd like changed, and
give the go‑ahead (a sign‑off section is at the end). **Section 7** records the content
decisions you've already confirmed; **Section 8** sets out a few further design proposals from
the manager review (two need PVAW sign‑off); **Section 9** covers the planned voiceover. The
only content item still to finalise is the exact statistics wording._

---

## 1. Purpose of the activity

A short, self‑paced, reflective web activity that supports our violence‑prevention work. It
follows one man — **Mr. Alpha** — through the pressures of migration and settlement, and invites
the player to help him respond as a leader. It rests on a simple idea:

> **The same pressures can grow very different outcomes. What changes the outcome is the
> beliefs a man holds, how he responds, and whether he leads.**

- **Audience:** migrant‑community men; EAL learners (simple English); male community leaders.
  Self‑paced, no login, works on a phone or computer.
- **Approach:** **primary prevention.** It is non‑judgemental — *"understand patterns, not
  blame people or culture."* It does not depict violence; it focuses on safety, equity and
  respect in the home as core values.
- **Tone:** hopeful and practical. The player is positioned as a **leader of positive change**,
  never lectured.

---

## 2. How it works — the tree

The whole activity uses one picture: **a tree**. As the story unfolds, the tree on screen
changes to reflect what is happening.

| Part of the tree | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **Roots** | Life pressures (migration stress, money, loss of status) |
| **Soil** | Beliefs and attitudes |
| **Trunk** | Behaviour |
| **Branches** | The impact on the family |

The activity has **two phases**:

- **Phase 1 — Experience the problem.** We follow Mr. Alpha as pressure builds. The tree
  **slowly declines** — leaves yellow, brown and fall; branches thin; the sky greys.
- **Phase 2 — Rebuild the outcome.** The player helps Mr. Alpha respond differently. Based on
  their choices, the tree **heals** — new green growth, brighter light — to one of three
  endings.

A support line — **1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) · in an emergency 000** — appears on **every
screen**, never hidden behind a "good" path.

## 3. The journey, step by step

### Opening

**Welcome.** A mature but slightly strained tree. Short framing in simple English:

> *Moving to a new country changes many things — work, language, culture, and family life. This
> can bring hope, and also stress. These pressures shape how people feel and act at home.*
>
> *Meet Mr. Alpha. He arrived in Australia in 2023. Back home, he was a respected community
> leader — people came to him for help. Now he is starting again. Follow his story, then help
> him lead a better way.*

A quiet note reminds the player this is about **understanding patterns, not blaming people or
cultures.**

### Phase 1 — the problem grows (the tree declines)

1. **Pressure · Money.** No work since arriving; money is tight; the home is too small.
2. **Pressure · A changing role.** His wife wants to study and work; his children want to
   choose their own paths; he feels he is losing his place as head of the family. *(These
   pressures are never presented as his fault — there is no "wrong answer" here.)*
3. **Two big pressures — an explainer.** A dedicated screen names the two pressures behind the
   story: **migration pressure** (a new language, hard to find work, money stress, few social
   connections) and **loss of status** (a respected leader now feeling unseen, "starting from
   zero," frustration and shame) — tied to Mr. Alpha, and reminding us they are **not his
   fault**. *(Included at the Education team's request, using Ali's ideas.)*
4. **Behaviour · What does he do?** The player chooses how Mr. Alpha responds under pressure —
   e.g. *take tight control of the money and decisions*, *go quiet and pull away*, or *let the
   stress come out as anger and blame*. Each is a realistic response; the game notes, gently,
   that understanding **why** this happens does not make it okay.
5. **Impact · Who feels it?** His partner feels stressed and can't follow her goals; the
   children feel worried and grow quiet; Mr. Alpha feels more alone. The tree is visibly
   declining.
6. **Belief · What sits behind it?** The player reflects on the belief underneath — e.g. *"As
   the father, it is my job to lead and decide."* A reflection, not a test.
7. **The whole tree.** The model is revealed: roots = pressures, soil = beliefs, trunk =
   behaviour, branches = impact. *"Pressures shape how we think, which shapes how we act, which
   affects the people around us. To make change, we look at the whole tree — not just the
   behaviour."* This closes Phase 1.

### Phase 2 — rebuilding (the tree heals)

8. **Now you lead.** The pressures are still there — but new beliefs and actions can grow a
   healthier tree.
9. **Attitude (the soil).** The player chooses a healthier belief — e.g. *"Respect is earned
   through trust, not control,"* or *"My worth is more than my job or my authority."*
10. **Behaviour (the trunk).** The player chooses the action a leader takes — e.g. *share money
    and big decisions with his wife*, or *ask for help — English classes, job support,
    counselling.* Asking for help is framed as a strength.
11. **Impact (the branches).** The family feels the change: his partner feels respected and can
    follow her goals; the children feel safe and open up; Mr. Alpha feels less alone — a leader
    through care, not control.

### The outcome — one of three

The ending **follows the Phase‑2 rebuilding choices**, never the pressures themselves. Stronger,
power‑sharing, help‑seeking choices grow a healthier tree:

- **A healthy tree** — full green canopy. Communication is open and respectful, decisions are
  shared, the home feels calmer and safer.
- **A tree that is healing** — part green. Some things improved; some strain remains. *"Change
  is a journey — every step counts."*
- **A tree under strain** — sparse and brown. The old patterns held on. Framed as **a warning,
  never "game over"** — and always **reversible**.

Both non‑healthy endings offer **"Go back and rebuild"** so the player can try different choices
and see a different outcome.

### Closing

12. **What we learn.** A short reflection tied back to the tree: *"By changing his beliefs, Mr.
    Alpha can change his actions — and build a safe, respectful home,"* followed by four prompts
    — *What belief needs to change first? · What would a strong leader think here? · What action
    shows leadership, not control? · What home do we want for the family?*
13. **Why this matters.** The activity zooms out from one family to the wider issue. It opens
    with one line, then a **"Show the numbers" button** the player can choose to open — the key
    statistics appear only on that click (opt‑in, so they land as understanding rather than a
    shock), with a hopeful close — *men can lead that change.* **(Figures and sources to be
    confirmed — see Section 7.)**
14. **Your turn to lead.** The player writes one small, real leadership action of their own —
    the pledge is what makes it theirs.
15. **Support & next steps.** The AMES *Stop Violence Against Women* leadership course,
    Neighbourhood Houses, local men's groups, and community/faith groups — plus the 1800RESPECT
    / 000 line.

## 4. The four key messages

The activity is designed to land these, without ever stating them as slogans:

1. Violence is not caused by culture alone.
2. Gender inequality, pressure and systems all matter.
3. Understanding causes helps prevent harm — it never excuses it.
4. Men can lead positive change.

## 5. How your Blueprint feedback has shaped the design

We worked through the review comments in your document. The key design decisions are:

- **Introduction** — opens by normalising migration/settlement as a period of stress and change
  (not with statistics), per Anu's guidance.
- **Scenario** — uses the softer, approved "alternative story": **no depicted violence or
  assault**, framed on safety, equity and respect.
- **Tone** — the causal chain stays gentle ("pressures → how we think → how we act → those
  around us"), and belief statements are toned down, per Anu.
- **Beliefs & attitudes** — combined into one step ("the soil") for simplicity, as the Edu team
  confirmed.
- **Leadership model** — Phase 2 mirrors the "Leadership Growth Model" (respect not control,
  sharing power, help‑seeking, a safe and respectful home).
- **Reflection prompts** — the four questions above are included in the takeaways.
- **Migration pressure / loss of status** — a **dedicated explainer screen** (Phase 1, step 3),
  using Ali's ideas and tied to Mr. Alpha.
- **Statistics** — placed at the **end**, per Anu.

---

## 6. Safeguards in the design

- Pressures and impacts are **never** a "wrong answer" — a man is not blamed for migration
  stress, money, isolation or loss of status.
- **Help‑seeking always helps**; harmful responses never "heal" the tree.
- **No outcome is caused by the pressure itself** — outcomes follow the rebuilding choices.
- The **damaged** ending is always reversible; the player can always try again.
- The **support line is on every screen**. No shame or scoring language anywhere.

## 8. Further design proposals (from the manager review)

A review with the manager raised a few additions. **Two of them touch how harm is depicted and
would need PVAW (Anu) sign‑off before we build them**, because they revisit the "no depicted
violence" boundary.

### A clearer link to family‑violence prevention — the "spark" *(pending PVAW sign‑off)*

At the moment the story is about stress and control, and the closing is about the wider issue —
the bridge between them isn't shown. We propose a **subtle, metaphorical** cue that unhealthy
responses and control, if left unaddressed, **can escalate into harm** — *without* portraying
Mr. Alpha as a villain or depicting any assault. The proposal is a **"spark": lightning strikes
the *tree* and sets it alight** (it chars and smokes — tree only, never a person), with framing
such as *"Sometimes it only takes a spark, and everyday stress can turn into harm. This is what
we work to prevent."* It would always be paired with **"Go back and rebuild"** (which puts the
fire out and heals the tree) and the support line.

### An escalating negative pathway (Phase 2) *(pending PVAW sign‑off)*

We propose that if the player keeps choosing control instead of returning to a healthier choice,
the experience **escalates**: the wording shifts to reflect **different ways people justify
harmful behaviour** (*"It's my house, my rules"* → *"I'm only protecting them"* → …), and the
**tree worsens with each step**, building to the "spark." This would show harm as **repeated
choices escalating over time**, and would stay **reversible at every step**.

> **Why these need PVAW:** both revisit the exact line Anu drew (primary prevention, no depicted
> violence). Done **metaphorically** (tree only, always reversible, support line present) they
> can *strengthen* the prevention message while staying non‑graphic — but Anu should approve the
> **approach and the wording** before we build. The wording can be adjusted easily.

### A figure of Mr. Alpha

We propose a simple **figure of Mr. Alpha** at the base of the tree, to give visual learners
extra context (a manager suggestion). It would be **drawn in the browser — no image files** —
so the activity stays self‑contained. His **emotional state would mirror the tree's health**:
his face moving from **hopeful → sad (under pressure) → tense** and back to calm, with a mood
"aura" overhead (a grey cloud, softening to a warm glow as he recovers). He would appear through
the **story screens (Phase 1 and the rebuild)** and step aside for the outcome/wrap‑up so the
tree stays the focus.

### Statistics behind a button

A small refinement (already reflected in Section 3, step 13): the closing statistics sit behind
a **"Show the numbers"** button, so they are **opt‑in** rather than shown up front.

## 9. Voiceover & narration (planned)

To support learners with **limited English literacy** or who prefer listening, we plan to
include **spoken narration** — a **"listen" button** that reads each screen aloud, **off by
default**.

- **Voice:** we plan to use **recorded voiceover** — clearer and warmer than a synthetic browser
  voice — recorded per screen in a warm, calm, unhurried delivery suited to EAL listeners.
- **Hosting (recommended):** the audio would be served as **direct audio files** delivered with
  the activity — **not** embedded video (for example, **not** Vimeo). Direct files give precise,
  instant play/stop for each screen and keep the security footprint small (a small "media"
  allowance in the site's security policy). Embedding a video player to carry audio is heavier,
  clunkier to control, and a wider security ask — so we'd avoid it.
- **Note:** recorded audio does mean a small set of **external files**. This is the one place the
  activity steps slightly away from being fully self‑contained, so it's worth confirming hosting
  with the **Digital team** and the **Drupal vendor** early.
